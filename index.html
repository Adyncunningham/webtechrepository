Thoughts on Progressive Enhancement

Before reading the article that we were assigned, I didn't know much about progressive enhancement. Upon reading it, I found that not only did the idea resonate with me heavily, but I was already familiar with it due to this class. Progressive enhancement is an approach to web design which focuses on the content created rather than the browser. It also emphasizes the importance of accessibility and functionality. This is contrasted against the graceful degradation approach which focuses on building for the most advanced browsers, with consequences for browsers which have issues with accessibility. This is crucial to not only consumer based websites but also work related ones aswell. This isn't to say that there are no potential flaws with the approach, just that it answers many problems that web development can face.

After reading this article I searched for more information about progressive enhancement and found this article. In this article by geeks for geeks they go into the philosophy and how the approach relates to the use of HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. They do this not only by describing the approach but also giving examples of its use across the three coding languages. This gave me more insight and helped me understand why the approach is important to modern web design. In my opinion, the approach is crucial because it ensures that the web is moving towards the goal of being universally accessible. This accessibility has become even more important as the web has scaled in size and user base, as more people rely on the site for work, commerce, and socialization.

Mars Final Site Reflection

Throughout the semester, I have found this class extremely enjoyable. I figured this would be the case early on, as it reminded me of the programming class I had enjoyed in the fall. It followed many of the same ideas early on, focusing on the semantics and programming sides of things. This would change rather quickly, focusing on why we program websites. This approach was interesting and would make me think differently about my work during the CSS demo and the following NASA project.

When creating this project, I tried to keep applying these principles, keeping in mind the audience and how that affects the expectations of the site. I would use things such as the summary tag and slick carousel to make the site look interesting, keep information precise, and make things more engaging. I showed this site to my friends who told me to remove the image under my carousel as it added nothing, to make the carousel slower, to center-align the footer text, and make the footer “look more interesting.”

I first addressed the issues of the image and the carousel, as they were the easiest to fix. The changes, I felt, made the homepage feel more streamlined and professional. This helped it reach the goal of connecting with my audience. Then I aligned the footer, which was just a small CSS change, to maintain consistency. Finally, I made the footer “look more interesting.” I wasn’t sure how to do this at first, then I remembered the SVG backgrounds from the CSS demo, implementing this allowed me to maintain professionalism while also making the site look more visually interesting. 

Overall, I’m proud of the site. I think that if I were to start over, I wish I would’ve experimented more with JavaScript and the organization of the website. This is because the experimentation, even if not used, could have still given me a better understanding of the tools I am using and more ideas design-wise. I feel I made things concise and addressed the audience adequately, but I wonder what other changes could’ve improved the message to the audience.
