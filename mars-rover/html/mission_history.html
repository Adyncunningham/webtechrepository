Mars Rover Mission History

NASA’s Mars Rover Program represents decades of innovation and discovery. Each mission has expanded our understanding of Mars, from early exploration to advanced scientific research searching for signs of ancient life.

Sojourner (1997)

The Sojourner rover was part of NASA’s Pathfinder mission and became the first successful rover to operate on another planet.

  • Launch: December 4, 1996
  • Landing: July 4, 1997
  • Location: Ares Vallis
  • Mission Goal: Demonstrate rover technology and analyze Martian rocks
  • Key Achievement: Proved that mobile exploration on Mars was possible

Spirit (MER-A)

Spirit was designed to explore Mars’ geology and search for evidence of past water activity.

  • Launch: June 10, 2003
  • Landing: January 4, 2004
  • Location: Gusev Crater
  • Mission Goal: Study rocks and soil for signs of past water
  • Key Discovery: Evidence of hydrothermal activity and ancient water environments

Opportunity (MER-B)

Opportunity far exceeded its expected lifespan and provided some of the strongest evidence of water on Mars.

  • Launch: July 7, 2003
  • Landing: January 25, 2004
  • Location: Meridiani Planum
  • Mission Goal: Search for past water activity
  • Key Discovery: Found hematite “blueberries,” indicating long-term water presence

Curiosity (2012–Present)

Curiosity is a car-sized rover designed to assess whether Mars ever had conditions suitable for life.

  • Launch: November 26, 2011
  • Landing: August 6, 2012
  • Location: Gale Crater
  • Mission Goal: Determine habitability of Mars
  • Key Discovery: Found organic molecules and evidence of ancient lakes

Perseverance (2021–Present)

Perseverance is NASA’s most advanced rover, focused on finding signs of ancient microbial life and collecting samples for future return to Earth.

  • Launch: July 30, 2020
  • Landing: February 18, 2021
  • Location: Jezero Crater
  • Mission Goal: Search for biosignatures and collect samples
  • Key Achievement: First rover to deploy a helicopter (Ingenuity)
