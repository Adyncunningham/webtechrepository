Frequently Asked Questions

Why does NASA send rovers to Mars?

NASA sends rovers to Mars to study the planet’s surface, climate, and geology. These missions help scientists understand whether Mars ever supported life and prepare for future human exploration.

How do Mars rovers communicate with Earth?

Mars rovers communicate using radio signals sent to orbiters around Mars, which then relay the data back to Earth. This process can take between 5 and 20 minutes depending on planetary positions.

What powers the Mars rovers?

Earlier rovers like Spirit and Opportunity used solar panels, while newer rovers like Curiosity and Perseverance use nuclear-powered generators (RTGs) that provide long-lasting energy.

How long do Mars rovers last?

Rovers are designed to last months or years, but many exceed expectations. Opportunity, for example, was designed for 90 days but operated for nearly 15 years.

Have Mars rovers found life?

No direct evidence of life has been found yet. However, rovers have discovered strong evidence that Mars once had water and conditions that could have supported microbial life.

What is Perseverance currently doing?

Perseverance is exploring Jezero Crater, collecting rock samples, and searching for signs of ancient life. These samples may eventually be returned to Earth by future missions.

What is Ingenuity?

Ingenuity is a small helicopter that traveled with Perseverance. It became the first aircraft to achieve powered flight on another planet, demonstrating new exploration possibilities.

Did You Know?

Mars is often called the "Red Planet" because of its reddish appearance, caused by iron oxide (rust) on its surface.
